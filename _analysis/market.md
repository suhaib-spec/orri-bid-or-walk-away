**Expected annual loss:**
- EIA's Monte Carlo model (built on 1995–2008 storm statistics) gave a **mean of 12.9 MMbbl per season, a median of 4.5 MMbbl**, a 90th percentile of 38.6 MMbbl and a 95th percentile of 50.7 MMbbl. It put a 19% chance on more than 25 MMbbl and a 5% chance on more than 50 MMbbl. EIA STEO Supplement, Jun 2009 (link above).
- Against 2018 GoM output of about 1.7 MMb/d (about 620 MMbbl/yr), that is a **mean of about 2% and a median of about 0.7%** of annual production (est.).
- A simple 2004–2017 sum (Ivan + K/R + G/I + Isaac + Harvey/Nate, about 290 MMbbl over 14 years at about 500 MMbbl/yr) gives **about 4%/yr including 2005, and about 1.8%/yr excluding 2005** (est.).
- Most shut-ins are *deferrals*: the barrels are produced later, so the cost to a royalty owner is the time value plus any price change. Permanent loss comes from facility destruction, mostly on shelf platforms (MP 59, SS 219, GI 116 are the shelf exposure here). Deepwater hosts can also suffer long outages (e.g. the Mars TLP after Katrina) or export-pipeline damage.
- Suggested modelling: haircut volumes by **1.5–3%/yr** or run a severe-year scenario. For example, 1 year in 10 loses about 25% of annual volume, and shelf fields carry a small chance of early abandonment.

### 4c. Insurance
I found no published product that offers business-interruption or loss-of-production cover to a non-operating ORRI holder. Operators and working-interest owners buy OEE and windstorm cover, but a royalty owner has no insurable interest in the facilities and receives nothing during a shut-in. Treat hurricane deferral as uninsurable and diversify or price it in instead. This is my judgment and is not sourced.

### BOEM/BSEE rules on ORRI holders and decommissioning
- Under **30 CFR 250.1701**, the parties jointly and severally liable for decommissioning are **lessees, owners of operating rights and their predecessors**, right-of-way holders, and right-of-use and easement holders. **ORRI owners are not listed.** https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/30/250.1701
- Record-title duties under **30 CFR 556.604** likewise attach to record-title owners, not overriding royalty owners. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/30/556.604
- Conclusion: an ORRI holder carries **no P&A or supplemental-bonding liability**. However:
  - An ORRI is carved out of the lessee's working interest and **is extinguished if the lease terminates**, unless the assignment has an anti-washout clause.
  - BOEM can require lessees to create an ORRI or production-payment stream to fund decommissioning accounts under 30 CFR 556.904(c). This is a risk only for future ORRIs and does not affect existing ones.
  - A financially weak operator can bring an early shut-in or abandonment decision, which ends the ORRI cash flow early.
  - In operator bankruptcy, ORRI treatment as a real-property interest versus an executory claim varies by state law. Louisiana law generally treats ORRIs as real rights (incorporeal immovables), but get legal confirmation.

## 6. Taxes
- **No state severance tax on federal OCS production.** OCSLA, 43 U.S.C. §1333(a)(2)(A), states: "State taxation laws shall not apply to the outer Continental Shelf." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/43/1333
  - Louisiana's 12.5% oil severance tax and Texas's 4.6% oil / 7.5% gas severance taxes apply only to state lands and state waters. State waters extend 3 nm for Louisiana and about 9 nm (3 marine leagues) for Texas.
  - All of the package's leases are OCS-G federal leases (BOEM filing above), so model **no severance or ad valorem tax**. The federal lessor royalty (12.5–18.75%) is borne by the lessee and is not deducted from the ORRI.
  - Check the ORRI instruments for whether post-production costs (transport, processing) are deductible.
- **Income tax:** the purchase price is recovered through **depletion**.
  - Cost depletion uses the units-of-production method on the allocated basis.
  - **Percentage depletion of 15%** of gross income is available to independent producers and royalty owners. It is capped at 1,000 bbl/d equivalent and limited to 100% of property net income and 65% of the taxpayer's taxable income. IRC §613A: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/613A
  - Integrated majors and large refiners are not eligible. My understanding is that the 1975 rule denying percentage depletion on transferred proven properties was repealed in 1990; confirm with a tax adviser.
  - Corporate rate 21% from Jan 1, 2018 (TCJA). Pass-through funds pass depletion through to their investors.

## 7. Discount rates and reserve-risk conventions (2018)
- The **SPEE Annual Survey (June 2018, 266 responses)** found risk-adjusted discount rates generally in the **10%–27%** range across reserve categories. PUD medians were a **60% reserve adjustment factor (RAF)** and a **20% risk-adjusted discount rate (RADR)**. A 60% RAF is roughly equivalent to a 15.25% RADR. PDNP is often taken at a 100% RAF. Mercer Capital, Dec 4, 2018 (published after the case date; the survey data is June 2018): https://mercercapital.com/insights/blogs/energy-valuation-insights-blog/2018/accounting-for-risk-in-oil-and-gas-reserve-valuations/ ; https://spee.org/annual-spee-parameter-survey
- **Bank conventions:** PV-9 or PV-10 on a bank price deck. Advance rates of PDP 65%, PDNP 45%, PUD 25% (Citi/SPEE 2019, above).
- **Typical A&D practice, as I would frame it for 2018:**

| Reserve class | Typical discount rate | Typical risk factor | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| PDP | 10–12% | 100% | |
| PDNP | 12–15% | 75–95% | |
| PUD | 15–20% | 50–75% | |
| Probable | 20%+ | 25–50% | |
| Possible | 20%+ | 10–25% | |

  - The same framework has a sponsor running 10–20% overall, which brackets the case's stated 10–20%.
  - Offshore ORRIs warrant a premium over onshore PDP for concentration, hurricane, single-host and operator risk. A reasonable range is +2–5 points, i.e. **about 12–15% for PDP** (my judgment).
- **SPE-PRMS** defines 1P/2P/3P as P90/P50/P10 probability thresholds for probabilistic estimates. Risked values are calculated as probability of commerciality times probability of discovery, applied to contingent resources and prospects. Reserves themselves are not chance-risked under PRMS; any discount comes from the buyer's own RAFs.
