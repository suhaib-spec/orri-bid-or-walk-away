# Part 2: The whole project in plain English

### The situation

We pretend it is **summer 2018**: the bid instructions came out in July, and the decision date is **August 15, 2018**. **BP**, the big oil company, is selling a bundle of **19 royalty interests** on oil and gas fields in the **Gulf of Mexico**, the ocean south of Texas and Louisiana. Bids are due **August 15, 2018**. We have **five weeks** to decide whether to **bid or walk away**, how much to pay, and where the money comes from.

### What is being sold?

An **overriding royalty interest (ORRI)** is the right to a small **percentage of the money from every barrel of oil and every unit of gas** sold from a field.

- Think of owning a few cents of every dollar a lemonade stand makes, **without paying for the lemons, the cups or the stand.**
- The royalty owner pays **no drilling, operating or clean-up costs**.
- The royalty owner also has **no control**. The oil companies that run the fields (the **operators**) decide when to drill, repair or shut down.

So we would be buying a **stream of future checks**. Their size depends on three things: **how much oil comes out**, **the oil price**, and **what percentage we really own**.

### What we found

**1. Three fields are almost all the value (slide 2).** Kodiak, Lucius and Stones hold about **95%** of the value. **Kodiak alone is about half, and it is one single well.** If that well breaks, a big part of the value disappears. That is our biggest risk.

**2. The fine print shrinks the package (slide 3).** We read the legal documents. Five royalties look bigger on BP's list than they are:
- **Lucius** looks like 2% + 2% = 4%. In January 2018 the neighbouring leases were combined into one "unit", and each lease gets an agreed share of the whole field. Our 2% applies only to each lease's share: 2% x 24.77% + 2% x 46.67% = **1.43%**. That is about **2.8 times** less.
- **Pompano** shares its name with a big field in BP's industry report, worth about **$71M**. Our royalty covers a **different area** that has produced **nothing since 2014**. Real value: about **$0**.
- **Ship Shoal** is listed at 8%. The company that created it (Amoco) owned only half of the lease, so we value it at **4%** and ask BP to confirm.
- **Stones** covers 8 blocks, but **only one block produces**.
- **Kodiak** is listed on two lines, but it is **one** royalty. Counting it twice would double the biggest asset.

**3. BP's past numbers are real, but its forecast is too high (slide 4).** BP says the package earned **$20.4M in 2017**. We rebuilt that from **government production records** and got **$20.6M**, within 1%. But 2017 is a poor guide to the future. Kodiak was **offline for about 8 months**, Lucius changed how it shares output, and Stones **shut down** from March to May 2018. The industry forecast in BP's data room (from **WoodMac**) is well **above** what actually came out in January to May 2018. Most of the gap is **future drilling**, and the case says **we cannot assume when drilling will happen**.

### What is it worth? (slide 5)

We valued **only the wells producing today**. The result is about **$102M in today's dollars** (base case). In our **optimistic case** (we call it the "operator trend": slower declines and no downtime), it is about **$134M**. If you trusted the seller's forecast you would get **$220M or more**, and that is the trap. Our **$98M** bid sits **below** our $102M value.

### The clever part: pay on proof (slides 6 and 7)

We bid **$98M cash** plus **up to $24M** in later payments. We pay those only if **free government production reports** show:
- **Kodiak keeps producing** above set levels in 2019, 2020 and 2021, with oil at $55 or more: **$5M a year, $15M in total**;
- a **second Kodiak well**: **$5M**;
- a **new Stones well**: **$4M**.

**Why this wins:** BP sees a headline of **up to $122M**. If BP believes its own forecast, it expects to collect most of it. If BP is wrong, we paid $98M and still earn about **15% a year**. In our own optimistic case (slower declines, no downtime), we earn about **26%**, even after paying BP about **$15M**. Nobody argues about whether a trigger was met, because the data is public. If BP refuses this structure, our fixed-price **maximum is $105M**.

### Where does the money come from? (slide 8)

The case says **the bank said no** and an investor said **five weeks is too short**. Our plan does **not need a bank to close**:
- We need about **$84M** on closing day, not $98M. The deal is backdated to January 1, 2018, so BP credits us about **$17.5M** of royalties it already collected for January to June. We also add **$3.1M** for costs and a hurricane reserve.
- We **assume** **95% ($79.4M)** comes from an **existing royalty fund** with committed capital. No fund has signed; that is why Aug 8 is our gate. **5% ($4.2M)** comes from our team.
- A fund can decide in weeks because **our research is already done** and there is **no drilling money** to commit.
- **No signed commitment by August 8 means we do not bid.**
- **After** closing we can optionally borrow **$25-30M** at **7.25%** (the **5.00% prime rate** + 2.25%). The deal does not depend on it.

### What could go wrong? (slide 9)

1. **The Kodiak well fails** in 2020: about **-$21M** of value. Our return dips slightly below zero (**-2%**).
2. **Oil falls to $45**: about **-$18M**. We **hedge**, meaning we lock in 2019-20 oil prices at about **$60-64**.
3. **Lucius pays the old way**: about **-$7M**. We ask BP for payment records.
4. **Hurricanes**: we cut forecast production by **2% every year**.

### Other bidders and questions for BP (slide 10)

Some bidders will believe the brochure and overpay. Some will multiply 2017 revenue and miss the 2018 changes. Disciplined royalty funds are our real competition. Before committing, we ask BP six sets of questions, starting with payment records from all 22 companies that pay the royalties.

### How we used AI (appendix)

AI read 60+ legal PDFs, pulled government data, drafted the model and role-played tough critics. **AI made real mistakes**: it said Lucius was 4%, it matched Pompano to the wrong field, and its "auction strategist" told us to bid $132M. We caught each mistake by checking the source documents. **All decisions were ours.**

# Part 3: Glossary (every term on the slides)

### Basics

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **BP** | The seller. A large oil company. |
| **Gulf of Mexico** | The ocean south of Texas and Louisiana. A major US oil region. |
| **Royalty / ORRI (overriding royalty interest)** | A fixed % of the money from all oil and gas sold. No drilling, operating or clean-up costs. No control. **This is what we are buying.** |
| **19 royalty interests / "slices"** | The 19 separate royalties in BP's package, on 9 fields. |
| **Field** | An underground pool of oil and gas, with the platform that produces it. |
| **Well** | A hole drilled to bring oil up. **Kodiak is one well.** |
| **Block** | A square on the offshore map, about 3 x 3 miles. Stones covers 8 blocks. |
| **Lease** | The government's permission to drill an area. |
| **Operator / "the operators"** | The oil companies that actually run each field (e.g. Shell runs Stones). |
| **Drilling, operating, clean-up costs** | Costs to drill wells, run the platform, and remove it at the end. **Royalty owners pay none of them.** |
| **Platform outage (3-6 months)** | A hurricane can stop a host platform for months. All royalties on it stop paying. |
| **Thousand barrels a day** | Speed of oil output. A barrel = 42 gallons. "Whole field" = all owners' oil, not just our share. |
| **Shut in** | Production stopped (repairs, storms, problems). Stones was shut in March to May 2018. |
| **Decline (35%, 25%, 22%)** | Wells produce less every year. A 35% decline means output falls about 35% in the first year; the drop slows after that. |
| **Hurricanes / output cut 2%/yr** | Storms can shut platforms for months. We cut forecast output 2% every year to allow for this. |

### Value and returns

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Value today / present value (PV) / "PV @12%"** | All future royalty checks converted into today's dollars. "@12%" = using a 12% discount rate. |
| **Discount rate** | How much less money is worth when it arrives later. At 12%, $112 next year is worth about $100 today. The case says use 10-20%. We use **12%**. |
| **Cost of money** | What our investors expect to earn: about 12% a year. A deal must beat it. |
| **Base case** | Our main, cautious forecast, built from public monthly data. |
| **Optimistic case ("operator trend")** | **Our own** optimistic case: Kodiak declines 22% and Lucius 25% a year, no downtime, existing wells only. It is **not** a forecast the operators gave us, and it is **not** WoodMac's forecast (that one gives ~$220M+). |
| **Seller's forecast** | WoodMac's forecast in BP's data room. Using it gives ~$220M+ of value. |
| **Possible new drilling (~$13M)** | Value of wells that might be drilled, reduced for the chance they never are. **Not in our price.** |
| **Fixed-price ceiling** | The most we'd pay as one all-cash number: $105M = $102M + a quarter of the $13M drilling value. |
| **Oil futures (Aug 15, 2018)** | Prices traders agreed on that day for oil delivered in future months. About $60-64 a barrel for 2019-20. Our price forecast. |
| **IRR (yearly return)** | The yearly return the investment earns, like an interest rate. Ours: ~15% base case. |
| **Before any loan** | Returns calculated without borrowing. We don't rely on a bank. |
| **Profit ~$31M / 1.3x money back / paid back by 2022** | Base case at $98M: total cash in minus cash paid = ~$31M; we get back $1.30 for every $1; our cash is fully returned by 2022. |
| **Scenario** | One version of the future (e.g. "$45 oil", "Kodiak fails in 2020"). |
| **Simulations (6,000)** | Running the model 6,000 times with random but realistic production inputs (Monte Carlo). |
| **Middle outcome / "1 in 10 below"** | Half the simulations are above the middle (~$98M). Only 10% are below ~$79M. |
