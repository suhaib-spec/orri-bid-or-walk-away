SLIDE 1 - "Bid or Walk Away? Our answer: BID"   (Speaker A, 1:10)
------------------------------------------------------------

Good morning. We are [team name]. I'm [name A], joined by [name B] and [name C].

An overriding royalty is a share of the revenue from every barrel a field produces, with no exposure to drilling, operating or decommissioning costs. In July 2018, BP offered nineteen of these interests in the Gulf of Mexico. Valued on the seller's forecast, the package is worth more than two hundred and twenty million dollars. Our analysis shows that figure does not hold up against the public data.

Our recommendation is to bid, with discipline.

[point to $98M] We offer ninety-eight million dollars in cash at closing.

[point to green box] We add up to twenty-four million in performance payments, paid only if the government's public production reports confirm the additional volumes.

[point to $105M] If BP requires a single fixed price, our ceiling is one hundred and five million. Above that, we walk away.

[point along bottom row] We value the producing wells at approximately one hundred and two million, for a return of about fifteen percent a year. And if our funding partner has not signed by August eighth, we do not bid.

The principle behind our offer is simple: [read the quote] we can't assume when drilling happens, so we pay for it when the government data shows it happened.

SLIDE 6 - "The extra $24M is paid only if public data proves it"   (Speaker B, 0:50)
------------------------------------------------------------

This is the core of our offer. BP believes its assets are worth more than our valuation. Rather than negotiate over forecasts, we propose to pay for results.

[top row] Ninety-eight million in cash, plus up to twenty-four million in performance payments, for a headline value of up to one hundred and twenty-two million.

[green cards] Fifteen million if Kodiak sustains production through 2019, 2020 and 2021, with oil at fifty-five dollars or more. Five million for a second Kodiak well. Four million for a new Stones well.

[bottom line] If BP's forecast is right, BP is paid in full. If it is not, we have paid ninety-eight million and still earn about fifteen percent.

DELIVERY TIPS
------------------------------------------------------------
- Pause briefly after each key figure and each highlighted line; let the point register before moving on.
- Address the judges directly rather than the screen, and indicate one element of the slide at a time.
- State figures in rounded terms ("about fifteen percent"); exact values are in the model if asked.
- Maintain accuracy: the fund is an assumption; decline rates were set by judgement, not fitted curves; refer to nothing after August 15, 2018.
- If running long, omit the Ship Shoal and Stones lines on slide 3 (about 10 seconds).
