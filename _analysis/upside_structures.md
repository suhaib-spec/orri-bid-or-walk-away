# BP GoM ORRI package (EnergyNet, bids due 15-Aug-2018): overlooked value, deal structures, hedge levels, bid metrics

Information cutoff rule: only facts public on or before 15-Aug-2018 support the valuation. Anything later is tagged **HINDSIGHT – not usable** and is included only so the team can check its answers, not for the bid case.
"Est." means my estimate or a practitioner benchmark with no public source found. Verify these before putting them on a slide.

## A. Value others might overlook, field by field

### A1. Lucius (KC 874/875; Anadarko 48.9% WI, operator). THE BIG ONE: Hadrian North is inside the Lucius unit
- **Anadarko 4Q17 Operations Report (6-Feb-2018):** "In late-2017, the company entered into an agreement with partners to expand the Lucius unit to encompass the adjacent Hadrian North discovery. Development of the initial subsea tieback program commenced in January 2018, with first production expected in 2019. The initial development phase is targeting high-quality Pliocene reservoir sands with similar rock and fluid properties as the prolific Lucius producing wells." Source: https://mma.prnewswire.com/media/638853/APC_4Q17_OpsReport.pdf (p.9).
- **Anadarko 2Q18 Operations Report (31-Jul-2018):** "The first Hadrian North expansion well was spud in the 2nd quarter of 2018, with first production expected in 2019." It lists Lucius as KC 874/875/918/919 with APC WI 48.9%. Q2-18 GoM volumes were down 9% q/q because of "planned downtime to tie back future wells to platforms." Anadarko expects about $85M/yr of extra revenue and cost-sharing from production-handling agreements (PHAs) at Constitution, Marco Polo, Marlin **and Lucius**. Source: https://mma.prnewswire.com/media/725143/Anadarko_Petroleum_Corporation_2Q18_Ops_Report.pdf (pp.8-9).
- **Anadarko FY2017 10-K (Feb-2018):** the 8th Lucius development well was drilled and completed in 2017, and the Hadrian North tie-back was "sanctioned and development … commenced in 2018, with first production expected in 2019." https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000773910/000077391018000012/apc201710k-10k.htm
- **Implication. This corrects the brief's assumption.** Hadrian North wells are not a separate-lease tieback. They are inside the expanded Lucius unit. The 1-Jan-2018 re-unitization that cut the ORRI to an effective 1.4288% happened because the Hadrian North tracts and reservoirs were added. The dilution therefore buys a share of Hadrian North production from 2019. If our ~$117M "ceiling" applies 1.4288% only to WoodMac's legacy Lucius gross revenue, it takes the dilution without the offsetting new barrels. WoodMac usually models Hadrian North as its own asset. Action: apply 1.4288% to Lucius plus Hadrian North unit revenue from 2019, risked at 70-85% because the wells are sanctioned and drilling. Upside is est. +$10-25M PV10, depending on WoodMac's Hadrian North profile.
- **Buckskin (LLOG op., Repsol 22.5%; KC 829 etc.) is a genuine third-party tieback.** It was announced in Sep-2017 as a 6-mile tieback to Lucius, with first oil expected 2H-2019, Phase 1 of 2 wells, and drilling from Jan-2018. Buckskin volumes do **not** count toward the ORRI. They use Lucius capacity under a PHA, which supports host life and fixed-cost sharing (good for ORRI longevity). They could crowd out unit volumes only if the spar is at capacity. Lucius was in decline by 2018, so crowding is a low risk. Sources: https://www.offshore-mag.com/field-development/article/16800395/llog-progressing-buckskin-in-the-deepwater-gulf-of-mexico ; https://jpt.spe.org/llog-starts-production-buckskin-project (HINDSIGHT for the June-2019 first-oil date).
- 9th Lucius well (planned Q4-2018 per our diligence). It was actually drilled in Q3-2018 with 230 net ft of Pliocene pay. Source: Anadarko FY2018 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/773910/000077391019000009/apc201810k-10k.htm. **HINDSIGHT – not usable.**
- Hadrian North first production April 2019. **HINDSIGHT – not usable.** Source: https://www.oedigital.com/news/499192-inpex-exits-lucius-hadrian-north-fields-in-u-s-gulf-of-mexico
- Depth: BP's own later filing (below) shows the KC 874 ORRI runs "Surface to 23,000'". Lucius Pliocene/Miocene producing sands are well above that, so no deep-rights upside is expected.

### A2. Kodiak (MC 727/771; Deep Gulf Energy op.; 6.375% of unit; Devils Tower host)
- **Kosmos–DGE deal (announced 6-Aug-2018):** $1.225B ($925M cash plus $300M stock). It adds about 25,000 boe/d (~85% oil) and about 80 MMboe of 2P (at 30-Jun-2018), at about $15/boe 2P (equivalent to $49k per flowing boe/d). Effective date 1-Jul-2018, closing about end of Q3-2018, cash funded from Kosmos's RBL, with $200M of extra RBL commitments obtained. Sources: 8-K Ex 99.1 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001509991/000095010318009546/dp94348_ex9901.htm ; 8-K https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001509991/000095010318009546/dp94348_8k.htm . The public release does **not** mention Kodiak by name. The investor deck on kosmosenergy.com was blocked (403), so we could not retrieve it.
- **Overlooked angles:**
  1. **Natural strategic buyer.** Kosmos becomes Kodiak's operator and WI owner. Through DGE it is also operator of **South Santa Cruz (MC 563)**, which ties back to Blind Faith; Ridgewood and Kosmos list Kosmos as SSC operator. One counterparty would therefore be burdened by two of our ORRIs (Kodiak 6.375% and SSC 0.93%). Kosmos funds with an RBL and wants to start a dividend, so buying out royalty burdens is accretive to its "free cash flow yield" story.
  2. **Price discovery.** Kosmos paid about $15/boe 2P for **working interest** (it carries opex, capex and ARO). A cost-free ORRI barrel is worth more per boe. Kosmos also financed GoM assets with a bank RBL, which undercuts "banks won't lend offshore" as a blanket statement (see B).
- **2nd Kodiak well.** Nothing public by 15-Aug-2018 showed it drilled or online. A Kodiak infill well rig move was announced in Jan-2020, and the 2nd well came online **Feb-2021**. **HINDSIGHT – not usable** (sources: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200128005910/en/Gulf-of-Mexico-Drilling-Update ; WoodMac asset summary https://www.woodmac.com/reports/upstream-oil-and-gas-kodiak-mc-771-8788261). *Usable lesson:* in Aug-2018 an operator mid-sale is a classic cause of schedule slip. Model the 2nd well with a 12-24 month delay case and at most 50-70% risking. Do not pay full PV for "planned 2018".
- **Single-well HPHT concentration.** The 260-day 2017–Feb 2018 outage is the key downside. Risk the PDP at PV15-PV20 or apply an availability haircut (e.g., 85%).
- Depth: the ORRI covers MC 727/771 "Surface to 23,000'" per BP's later filing. Kodiak's deep Miocene HPHT pay sits around 25,000+ ft TVD (reported). **Verify in the data room** whether the producing interval is within the depth grant. If the producing sands lie below 23,000' TVDSS, the ORRI may not burden them; the filing language suggests the ORRI is "subject to the Kodiak Unit agreement." This is a critical title check.

### A3. Stones (Shell; Turritella FPSO; WR 508 producing; 1.5% ORRI on all 8 WR blocks)
- Pre-cutoff public plan: Phase 1 had 2 subsea wells (Stones-5R and Stones-9) online Sep-2016. The plan was "six further production wells will be added later" with subsea pumping. Eight subsea trees were ordered from FMC. The OneSubsea 15,000-psi pump system was "expected" to be delivered in 2018. Shell targeted about 50,000 boe/d at peak, and Turritella capacity is 60,000 b/d oil plus 15 MMcfd. Shell bought Turritella from SBM for about $1B (option exercised Jul-2017), which signals a long-life commitment to the host. Sources: https://www.offshore-mag.com/production/article/16769284/shell-starts-up-stones-in-the-ultra-deepwater-gulf-of-mexico ; https://www.ogj.com/drilling-production/article/17289654/shell-to-buy-fpso-serving-stones-project ; https://www.ogj.com/drilling-production/article/17229407/shells-stones-field-advances-subsea-production-technology
- **Overlooked value.** The ORRI burdens **all 8 WR blocks**, so it is a free option on every Phase-2 well wherever it is drilled. Most bidders will value WR 508 PDP only. Risked Phase-2 value: 6 wells at 30-60% risk and 2-4 years' timing. Est. +$10-30M PV10 on top of PDP if WoodMac's profile excludes Phase 2.
- 2018 uptime: SBM fleet uptime was 98.0% in 2018, with no Turritella-specific figure. **HINDSIGHT – not usable** (SBM 2018 AR). Disconnectable FPSO design means hurricane shut-ins are planned and short (oilnow/offshore-mag "designed for swift disconnection").
- A 1.5% ORRI across 8 blocks is a real **cost burden to Shell on marginal Phase-2 wells**, so Shell is a logical buy-back counterparty.

### A4. South Santa Cruz (MC 563; 0.93%; tieback to Chevron's Blind Faith)
- Operator: DGE, which becomes Kosmos after the 6-Aug-2018 deal. Partners include Houston Energy, Ridgewood, Red Willow, He&D, and Talos (via Noble/Stone history). First oil Jun-2017 (WoodMac). Initial well peaked at about 23,000 boe/d gross. **The peak figure is HINDSIGHT – not usable** (Kosmos 2018 AR, https://www.kosmosenergy.com/annualreport/2018/inc/html/32.html).
- ORRI depth: "surface down to 19,000' TVDSS" (BP later filing). Check whether the 3rd-well location (not in WoodMac) targets sands above 19,000'.
- **Bundle with Kodiak for a single Kosmos resale.**

### A5. Pompano (MC 72 S/2 & S/2 S/2 N/2; 5.625%; surface–13,405 ft)
- The Talos–Stone combination closed 10-May-2018 (Talos 10-Q Q2-18, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001724965/000156459018020821/talo-10q_20180630.htm). Talos has a $600M initial borrowing base on an offshore RBL.
- **Mt. Providence is on MC 28, not MC 72.** Stone announced the discovery on 10-Jan-2018: MC 28 #4, 100% Stone, about 153 ft net oil pay, tied back to the Pompano platform (https://www.oedigital.com/news/444615-stone-hits-pay-at-mt-providence). It came online in mid-2018 at about 3,850 boe/d gross (NGI). **It gives the MC 72 ORRI no direct value.**
- Talos's 2019 programme was at Main Pass 72 (MP 72, a different field), not MC 72. Nothing public shows LLOG, M21K or Nexen activity on MC 72 S/2. The Pompano platform has spare capacity (it hosts MC 28 tiebacks), so a shallow (<13,405') MC 72 tieback is cheap if someone drills it. Treat this as a **lottery ticket worth $0.5-2M**, not as base value.
- Deep Rampart sands are excluded by the 13,405' limit. This is correctly valued at zero.

### A6. Orion (MC 110) ~ $0. Shelf: GI 116, MP 59, SS 219
- MP 41/59 (Main Pass/Bay Marchand ex-Chevron). Cantium (York Capital / Sole Source backed) bought Chevron's shelf assets including Bay Marchand and Main Pass (300 wells, 151 platforms), closing 30-Jun-2017. A PE-backed operator buys shelf assets to run a recompletion programme, which is mild upside for the 2.6% MP 59 ORRI. Source: https://www.oedigital.com/energy/oil/item/15739-cantium-buys-chevron-gom-shelf-assets
- GI 116 (Fieldwood) and SS 219: shelf operators were under financial stress in 2018 (Fieldwood went through a prepackaged Ch.11 that closed Apr-2018 together with its $710M Noble deepwater purchase). **An ORRI carries no plugging liability**, so operator insolvency risks only revenue, not ARO. Keep the shelf at $5-10M, or sell it to a shelf specialist.

### A7. Critical title/depth items (data room check)
- BP's Jan-2025 BOEM non-required filing (assigning **25%** of these ORRIs to Five Stones Energy, eff. 1-Dec-2024) restates the depth limits from the original instruments:
  - Kodiak unit: "Surface to 23,000'"
  - KC 874: "Surface to 23,000'"
  - MC 563: "Surface down to 19,000' TVDSS"
  - It also confirms the Lucius unit (KC 874 SE/4, 875 S/2, 918, 919).
  - Source: https://data.boem.gov/PDFDocs/Scan/ADJUD/87/87222.pdf.
  - **HINDSIGHT – not usable for valuation.** It also implies that BP still held the Kodiak, Lucius, SSC and Orion ORRIs in 2024, i.e., the 2018 package likely did **not** trade, or not in full. Use this only as a sanity check that the seller's price expectations were high.
- BOEM treats ORRI assignments as "non-required filings" (Category 5, filed for record, no approval needed). **No regulatory approval gates the 14-Sep closing**, unlike WI deals such as Kosmos/DGE, which needed HSR.

## B. Deal structures under a 5-week, all-cash, no-bank constraint

Cost-of-capital reference (Aug-2018):
- Prime 5.00%; 3M LIBOR about 2.3% (est.).
- Offshore RBL about L+250-350, i.e., 5-6% all-in (est.). Offshore RBLs do exist: Talos $600M BB (May-2018) and Kosmos's RBL upsized by $200M for DGE (Aug-2018).
- Private credit or 2nd-lien about 9-12% (est.). Hard-money bridge about 10-15% plus 1-3 points (est.).
- Structured preferred about 7% cash coupon plus conversion (Apollo→Kimbell). Royalty or family-office equity has an unlevered IRR target of about 10-15% for PDP-heavy royalties (est.).

- **WTI front month:** Sep-18 settled $65.01 on 15-Aug-2018, down $2.03. Brent Oct-18 settled $70.76 (OGJ, 16-Aug-2018: https://www.ogj.com/articles/2018/08/market-watch-crude-oil-prices-fall-on-concerns-about-emerging-market-economies.html). Brent-WTI spread $6.62 (Sprague 20-Aug-2018).
- **WTI Cal-2019 ≈ $63/bbl.** Goldman's review of Q2-18 hedges, reported by Argus (article dated 5-Sep-2018, i.e., just after the cutoff), puts 2019 producer hedges at ~$60 vs a 2019 strip of ~$63: https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news/1748547-us-independents-raise-their-hedges . Talos's 2019 swaps were $54.14 (legacy positions, Q2-18 10-Q).
- **WTI Cal-2020 ≈ $58-60; Cal-2021 ≈ $56-58 (est.).** The curve was backwardated (RBN; DTN 21-Aug-2018; DailyFX 22-Aug-2018 says the Dec18-Dec19 spread was widening). An exact CME settlement could not be retrieved, so pull the CME settlement archive before presenting.
- **Henry Hub (NYMEX settlements 7-Aug-2018, Mansfield Energy Nat Gas News 8-Aug-2018):**

  - Jan-Aug 2020 average = $2.647, so Cal-2020 ≈ **$2.68** (est.).

## D. Typical 2018 bid metrics (producing royalties; offshore haircut)
- Kosmos/DGE (offshore WI): ~$15/boe 2P; $49k per flowing boe/d; Kosmos's own EV ~$21/boe.
- Fieldwood/Noble deepwater (WI with ARO): $480M cash / 20 kboe/d ≈ $24k per flowing boe/d, plus a $100M contingent payment; $710M total value incl. ARO. Source: https://www.offshore-mag.com/articles/2018/04/fieldwood-acquires-noble-energy-s-deepwater-gom-assets.html
- Kimbell/Haymaker (onshore minerals): implied FCF yield ~15%, i.e., ~6.7x cash flow (Mercer, 18-Jul-2018: https://mercercapital.com/details-and-analysis-of-kimbell-royalty-partners-acquisition-of-haymaker-minerals-royalties-llc). Public mineral companies traded at ~8.7-12% yields (8-11x).
- Discount rates: SPEE survey risk-adjusted rates of 10-27% (Mercer). Practitioner norms (est.):
  - PDP royalty at PV10-PV12 onshore, **PV12-PV15 for offshore PDP** (hurricane, single-well and host-outage risk).
  - PDNP/sanctioned tiebacks (Hadrian North) at 60-85% of PV.
  - PUD 25-50%; probable/possible 0-15%.
  - Cash-flow multiples for offshore ORRIs ~3.5-5.5x forward cash flow.
- **Package math:**
  - 2017 net production 400 Mbbl + 300 MMcf ≈ 450 Mboe ≈ 1,230 boe/d; revenue $20.4M (Hart).
  - At $100-150M: 4.9-7.4x 2017 revenue, or ~3.6-5.4x a 2018E ~$28M.
  - That equals $80-120k per flowing boe/d on 2017 volumes, i.e., 1.6-2.5x Kosmos's WI price per flowing barrel. A royalty premium over WI is normal (no opex/capex), but the top of the $150M range looks rich for offshore concentration risk.
