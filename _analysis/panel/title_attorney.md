## What the team missed or got wrong

**A. Stones royalty relief (the largest unpriced risk).**
- **The lease is royalty-suspended.** WR 508 is a Sale 157 lease in 2,919 m of water. Its lease addendum suspends lessor royalty for the first 87.5 MMBOE. *Kerr-McGee* (5th Cir. 2009) voided the price thresholds on leases issued 1996–2000.
- **The ORRI tracks the lessor's royalty.** It is "calculated in the same manner as Lessor's royalty" and paid "at the same time as the Lessor's royalty."
- **The other overrides protect against this; Stones does not:**
  - Kodiak: "as…if there had been no royalty relief"
  - KC 875: "whether or not royalties are due"
  - SSC: "shall not…bar, delay or diminish"
- **What is at stake.** Cumulative production is only about 10 MMBOE of the 87.5. A payor could argue the ORRI is suspended for years, which puts about $17M of Stones value at risk.
- **Mitigant.** The team's 2017 match to Hart's $20.4M only works if Stones was being paid (about $5.7M). Without Stones the match misses by 28%.
- **Ask for:** Shell remittances since Sep 2016 and a seller indemnity against recoupment.

**B. Ship Shoal 219: use 4%.**
- "Properties" means Amoco's 50% *interest*, and the ORRI runs on production "from the Properties." So 8% × 50% = 4%.
- Stones is different. There the ORRI runs on production from the whole "Assigned Lease," and the two fractions (1.26725% + 0.23275%) were engineered to total 1.5%.
- Treat the seller's 0.08 as a representation. If the payor pays 4%, the price drops by 50% of the allocated value, and the seller bears recoupment of past overpayments.

**C. Kodiak: the 260-day production gap.**
- The register records 2013–2016 suspensions of production, so the absence of one for Jun 2017–Feb 2018 matters. The likely basis is:
  - a 30 CFR 250.180 Regional Supervisor extension, or
  - continuing well-repair operations.
- BOEM kept treating the lease as alive. It entered operator designations through 19-Mar-2018, recorded "Production resumed 02/22/2018" and showed status UNIT on 4-Jun-2018 (`4. Serial Register Page MC 771`).
- **Odds and price:** termination odds are under 5%, about $1–2M of expected value.
- **Remedy:** a closing deliverable (the BSEE approval or operations record) plus a specific indemnity, not a price cut.
- **Option-value leak:** the well has reported on MC 727 since Jan 2017. MC 771 could drop out of the unit (Art. 13.1, about Feb 2019) and then expire.

**D. Main Pass 59.** The team values production *located on* the lease. But the ORRI is paid "in the same manner as the Lessor's royalty," which follows the unit allocation (Unit 754300007). The team applied exactly this logic to Lucius. The tract factors are unknown. The override also burdens only Amoco's old 50%, now spread across six depth-severed owners, so the risk of non-payment is high.

**E. Lucius points to tighten.**
1. **KC 874.** The creating instrument is missing. Four third-party recitals confirm the 2% ("2.0% each to CVX/UNOCAL and BP"). The KC 874 register (p1) shows Devon assigning record title effective 11/1/2009 while keeping deep rights, which fits a reservation at that time.
2. **KC 875.** Devon conveyed to BP, AS-IS, *before* Devon itself received the ORRI. After-acquired title is weak without a warranty. **Cure:** a Devon confirmatory assignment.
3. **Retroactive unit adjustment.** The unit was approved 5/21/2018 but is effective 1/1/2018. Jan–May payments were probably made on the lease-located basis (about 33% of liquids vs 71.44% under the unit). The roughly $2–3M true-up must go to the buyer.
4. **Exhibit C revisions** apply going forward (Art. 11.4). A ±10% change in factors moves value by about ±6.5%.
5. **Hadrian North.** The memo's "Hadrian North into Lucius unit" upside is wrong. Its tariff goes to working-interest owners, and adding Hadrian North to the participating area would dilute the tract factors. Delete it.

**F. Pompano: the team is right.**
- The only producing MC 72 completion bottoms outside the ORRI area: TB008D, in the N/2 S/2 N/2, operated by Talos (ex-Stone), not an assignee.
- Stone's 10% came from KMG, not from BP's assigned interest.
- No route reaches WoodMac's VK 989/990 or MC 27/28/29 production. Only the unseen 2007 farmout could widen coverage.

**H. Two seller entities.** The GI, MP and SS overrides stand in Amoco Production Co. (now BP America Production Co.); the rest in BP Exploration & Production Inc. Both must sign, and name-change evidence is needed.

**Recording.** BOEM neither requires nor approves ORRI transfers; filings are record-only. Notice to third parties comes from recording in the adjacent Louisiana parish. The buyer should record there, file with BOEM, and send letters-in-lieu to every payor.

**I. Bid Instruction requirements the team ignored.**
- Allocation must be "by individual interest": that is **19 lines**, not 9.
- There are no PSA, title, deposit or casualty terms, and closing comes only 15 days after award. The letter must supply these.
- The allocation becomes the remedy schedule. Weight it to the producing tracts:
  - all Stones value on WR 508,
  - Lucius KC 875/874 about 65/35,
  - Kodiak toward MC 727,
  - Grand Isle 110/116 split 19.27/80.73.

## Bid-letter protections
1. **Defensible Title, per interest:** at least the Attachment A decimal, on 8/8 of production *allocated* to the lease (unit basis where applicable), within the stated depths, with no unpaid payout, free of liens, **and actually being paid at that decimal**.
2. **Title-defect adjustment** = allocated value × (shortfall ÷ stated decimal), with a $25k per-defect threshold and no aggregate deductible. The buyer may exclude any interest with a defect above 50% of its allocated value. No netting of title benefits across interests.
3. **90-day post-closing title period**, backed by a **10% holdback (about $11M) for 12 months**.
4. **Special warranty**, plus subrogation to prior grantors' warranties.
5. **Representations:**
   - no prior conveyance or encumbrance,
   - no termination notices,
   - no payor disputes, recoupment or suspense (any suspense listed),
   - no unrecorded agreement that reduces or conditions any ORRI,
   - no preferential rights or consents.
6. **Closing deliverables:**
   - remittances and division orders for 2016–2018,
   - the KC 874 instrument,
   - a Devon confirmatory assignment,
   - Amoco→BP vesting documents,
   - the controlling agreements,
   - Kodiak lease-maintenance evidence,
   - a complete WR 551 copy,
   - BP's 2011 Stone excluded-assets list,
   - SSC recordation.
7. **Effective-time accounting:** all post-1/1/2018 proceeds go to the buyer, including Lucius retro adjustments and suspense. Pre-closing recoupment stays with the seller. Final settlement at 150 days.
8. **Right to drop interests** at their allocated values. The Bid Instruction allows separate bids.

## Effect on the bid
- **Change the model now:** Ship Shoal at 4% is about **−$1.5M**.
- **Expected-value haircuts unless proof arrives before award:**
  - Stones royalty relief: −$2M
  - Kodiak lease continuity: −$1M
  - Grand Isle depth and Main Pass unit basis: −$0.5M
  - SSC recordation: −$0.3M
- **Total: about −$5M.**

**Recommendation:** with protections 1–8 in the letter, keep the **$110M** headline. If the seller strikes them, bid **$105M** and cut the walk-away from $125M to **$120M**. Above $120M the buyer would be paying for unproven title upside: SS 219 at 8%, Stones paid through royalty relief, and the KC 874 terms.
